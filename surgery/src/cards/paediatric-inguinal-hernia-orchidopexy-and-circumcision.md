## What the surgeon is doing and why

Three operations share a patient group, a list and a nerve supply, and differ in what is pulled on.

Inguinal hernia in a child is not a weakness of the abdominal wall. The processus vaginalis, the finger of peritoneum that follows the testis into the scrotum, has failed to close, and bowel (or ovary) slides down it. The surgeon therefore does a herniotomy: a short skin-crease incision, the external oblique opened or the cord delivered at the external ring, the thin sac separated from the vas and vessels, twisted, tied flush at the internal ring and divided. No muscle is repaired and no mesh is used. A communicating hydrocele is the same lesion with a narrower neck and gets the same operation. Because the risk of incarceration is highest in the first months, infants are repaired promptly, and prematurity makes hernia far more common, which is how a 2.5 kg ex-premature infant comes to be on an elective day list. Laparoscopic repair closes the internal ring from inside with a suture and lets the surgeon inspect the other side.

Orchidopexy brings an undescended testis into the scrotum, usually at 6-18 months. Through a groin incision the testis and cord are freed, the processus (almost always patent) is separated and ligated high, the cord is mobilised into the retroperitoneum to gain length, and the testis is drawn through a tunnel into a pouch between dartos and scrotal skin made through a second incision. The step that matters to you is traction on the cord and peritoneum. An impalpable testis is found by laparoscopy and, if the vessels are too short, brought down in two stages months apart.

Circumcision removes the foreskin: the prepuce is freed from the glans, excised, and the skin sutured to the mucosal cuff, with bipolar diathermy to the frenular artery.

The pain logic follows the innervation. The groin wound is somatic, T12-L1, through the ilioinguinal and iliohypogastric nerves. The testis and cord carry visceral afferents to T10, so cord traction is felt above the level any groin block reaches. The scrotum is sacral (pudendal and posterior scrotal nerves) with an anterior contribution from the ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves. The penis is S2-4 through the dorsal nerves. Hence the mapping: a caudal covers all of it, an ilioinguinal block covers the hernia wound only, and a penile block covers the penis only. All three operations are skin and soft tissue and score 2; circumcision behaves differently afterwards because a raw edge is bathed in urine several times a day.

## Steps

### 1. Preassessment and the ex-premature infant
**Why:** The surgeon wants infant hernias fixed early because they incarcerate; that timing collides with the period of highest apnoea risk.

**Anaesthesia:** Establish gestation at birth and postmenstrual age today. Control of breathing is immature in the premature infant and anaesthesia unmasks it as apnoea, sometimes hours later. In the combined analysis of 255 former preterm infants, apnoea was inversely related to both gestational and postconceptual age, anaemia was an added risk factor, and the risk did not fall below 5% until 48 weeks and below 1% until 54-56 weeks postconceptual age. Most units therefore admit ex-premature infants under about 60 weeks postmenstrual age (some use 52) for at least 12 hours of apnoea and saturation monitoring, and term infants under about 44-46 weeks likewise. Check the haemoglobin, ask about ongoing apnoeas, home oxygen and caffeine, and put the infant first on the list. An incarcerated hernia that cannot be reduced is an emergency with a full stomach and possibly ischaemic bowel; one that has been reduced is repaired on the next list after 24-48 hours for oedema to settle.

### 2. Awake regional or general anaesthesia for the infant
**Why:** Open herniotomy in a small infant takes a practised surgeon 20-40 minutes, which is within the life of a spinal.

**Anaesthesia:** The question has been tested. In the trial of 722 infants randomised to awake regional or sevoflurane general anaesthesia, apnoea in the first 12 hours was similar (3% and 4%); regional anaesthesia reduced early apnoea in the first 30 minutes (1% against 3%), late apnoea was 2% in both arms, and almost all apnoeas were in the premature. Neurodevelopment at two and five years was equivalent, with a median general anaesthetic of 54 minutes. Two conclusions follow. An hour of sevoflurane in infancy does not measurably harm development, so parental fear of that is not a reason for a spinal. And awake regional anaesthesia buys a calmer recovery room, not a safe discharge: monitoring is needed either way. The price is failure: 74 of 363 infants allocated to regional had a protocol violation, against two in the general anaesthesia arm. The defensible default is general anaesthesia with a tracheal tube, a caudal and no opioid; an awake spinal (hyperbaric or plain bupivacaine 0.5%, 1 mg/kg, which is 0.2 mL/kg) is reasonable for a unilateral open repair by a quick surgeon in an infant with significant lung disease, with sucrose on a dummy and no sedative supplement.

### 3. Induction and airway for the term child
**Why:** Nothing surgical yet.

**Anaesthesia:** Clear fluids are allowed until one hour before, which keeps toddlers calmer and veins fuller. Inhalational or intravenous induction according to the child and the cannula. A supraglottic airway with spontaneous ventilation suits the term child over about 5 kg for open groin and penile surgery; a tracheal tube is for neonates, ex-premature infants, laparoscopic repair and the reduced-incarcerated hernia with a distended abdomen. Give paracetamol 15 mg/kg IV, or have given it orally with ibuprofen 10 mg/kg before theatre.

### 4. The block, before incision
**Why:** The surgeon waits two or three minutes; in return the child needs less volatile and no opioid and does not move at incision.

**Anaesthesia:** Match the block to the operation.

```
 Operation              Needs to cover           Block
 ---------------------  -----------------------  ---------------------------
 Unilateral herniotomy  T12-L1 groin wound       ilioinguinal or TAP (walks)
                                                 caudal (not yet walking)
 Orchidopexy            T10 cord, L1 groin,      caudal 1 mL/kg
                        sacral scrotum
 Bilateral groin        both sides, one dose     caudal 1 mL/kg
 Circumcision           S2-4 penis               dorsal penile nerve block
```

Caudal: levobupivacaine 0.25% or ropivacaine 0.2%, 0.5 mL/kg for sacral dermatomes and 1 mL/kg to reach T10, after negative aspiration and with the ECG watched during a slow injection. Ilioinguinal-iliohypogastric block under ultrasound needs about 0.2 mL/kg; by landmark it fails in 20-30% and can spread to the femoral nerve. Penile block: plain solution only, never with adrenaline, about 0.1 mL/kg each side of the midline beneath the deep fascia. Whatever the combination, add the surgeon's infiltration into the same total: 2-2.5 mg/kg of levobupivacaine. In more than 100,000 paediatric blocks in the registry there was no permanent nerve injury and severe systemic toxicity occurred in 0.76 per 10,000, most of them infants, which is the group in whom the calculation matters most.

### 5. Groin incision and finding the sac
**Why:** The surgeon opens the canal or delivers the cord, splits the cremaster and identifies the sac on the anteromedial side of the cord, peeling the vas and vessels off its back wall.

**Anaesthesia:** The test of the block. A rise in heart rate and respiratory rate, or movement, at skin incision 10 minutes after a caudal means it has missed or been given outside the canal: supplement with fentanyl 1 microgram/kg IV and ask the surgeon to infiltrate, and do not chase it with volatile alone in a spontaneously breathing child on a supraglottic airway, where the next event is laryngospasm.

### 6. High ligation and cord traction
**Why:** The sac is dissected to the internal ring, where preperitoneal fat appears, twisted to empty it, transfixed and divided. In orchidopexy the surgeon also pulls the testis and cord down while dividing lateral bands to gain length.

**Anaesthesia:** Traction on peritoneum and cord is the most stimulating moment in all three operations and it is visceral: an ilioinguinal block does not cover it, a caudal to T10 does. Expect breath-holding, laryngospasm or bradycardia in a lightly anaesthetised child. Deepen before the pull, not after, and have propofol 1 mg/kg IV drawn up. Ask the surgeon to release if the heart rate falls; atropine 20 micrograms/kg IV if it does not recover.

### 7. Laparoscopic herniotomy (variant)
**Why:** A camera at the umbilicus shows both internal rings; the open ring is closed with a purse-string or a percutaneous suture passed around it outside the peritoneum.

**Anaesthesia:** Tracheal tube, relaxant, pneumoperitoneum at 6-10 mmHg and head-down tilt: a longer and more physiologically active anaesthetic for the same pathology, exchanged for a look at the contralateral side and no cord dissection. In a small infant watch for endobronchial migration with tilt and for the fall in tidal volume as the abdomen fills (see [paediatric laparoscopic and thoracoscopic surgery](paediatric-laparoscopic-and-thoracoscopic-surgery.html)). Port sites are infiltrated; a caudal still works for the peritoneal and groin component.

### 8. Orchidopexy: the scrotal pouch
**Why:** A finger or clamp is passed from the groin to the scrotum, a pouch is made beneath the skin through a small scrotal incision, and the testis is drawn down and fixed in it.

**Anaesthesia:** The scrotal incision is sacral territory, outside an ilioinguinal block. If no caudal was given the surgeon infiltrates the scrotum first. Orchidopexy carries a higher rate of postoperative vomiting than herniotomy, attributed to cord traction: give ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg IV and dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV.

### 9. Circumcision
**Why:** The foreskin is retracted, adhesions freed, the prepuce excised by sleeve or guillotine technique, bleeding points coagulated with bipolar diathermy and the edges sutured.

**Anaesthesia:** Short, with a sharp stimulus at the frenulum. A penile block placed before the start covers the operation and the first hours; the ventral penile skin and frenulum are the usual patch, which a ring of infiltration at the base corrects. The Cochrane review of ten trials found no difference in rescue analgesia between caudal and penile block, and more leg weakness after caudal, so a penile block is preferred for any boy who walks. Adrenaline-containing solutions are never used on the penis. Monopolar diathermy is avoided on an end-artery appendage; that is the surgeon's rule, and worth noticing if the pad is being applied.

### 10. Closure and emergence
**Why:** Subcuticular absorbable sutures and glue; no dressing that needs removing.

**Anaesthesia:** With a working block the child needs little volatile at the end. Remove a supraglottic airway either deep or fully awake, not in between. Emergence delirium after sevoflurane is common at two to five years and is hard to distinguish from pain; a working block and a small dose of propofol or an alpha-2 agonist at the end reduce it. The ex-premature infant is extubated awake and goes to a monitored bed.

### 11. Recovery and discharge
**Why:** The surgeon expects a day case: the child should drink, be comfortable and, by local protocol, may go home before passing urine.

**Anaesthesia:** After a caudal, tell the parents the legs may be heavy for a few hours and that the first void may be delayed; most units no longer make voiding a discharge criterion. Regular paracetamol and ibuprofen for 48-72 hours, started before the block wears off, is the whole of the take-home plan. Circumcision needs advice about stinging on voiding for several days, loose clothing and a barrier ointment.

## Pain plan

The mechanism is skin and soft tissue in three different nerve territories, with a visceral component from cord traction in orchidopexy. All three are short-lived pains fully covered for the first 4-8 hours by a correctly chosen single-shot block, which is why the block choice is the plan.

Caudal is the most versatile: one injection covers groin, cord, scrotum and penis on both sides, it is reliable in infants, and at 1 mL/kg of 0.2-0.25% solution it reaches T10. Its costs are transient leg weakness and delayed micturition, which matter in a child who walks and not in one who does not. The defensible default is therefore caudal for orchidopexy at any age, for bilateral groin surgery, and for any infant; an ultrasound-guided ilioinguinal or transversus abdominis plane block for unilateral herniotomy in the walking child; and a dorsal penile nerve block for circumcision. For circumcision that default is supported by the Cochrane review; for hernia and orchidopexy it is author's judgement built on the anatomy, since trials comparing caudal with ilioinguinal block are small and show similar early analgesia.

A plain caudal lasts about four to six hours. Additives extend it: preservative-free clonidine 1-2 micrograms/kg or dexmedetomidine 1 microgram/kg prolong analgesia in meta-analysis without more adverse events, at the cost of some sedation. They are avoided in neonates and ex-premature infants because of apnoea. Ultrasound for the ilioinguinal block halves the volume needed and reduced supplementation from 26% to 4% in the randomised comparison with the landmark technique.

Systemic: paracetamol 15 mg/kg and ibuprofen 10 mg/kg (not under three months), given before or during surgery and then regularly for two to three days. Opioid is rarely needed with a working block; when a block has failed, fentanyl 0.5-1 microgram/kg IV in recovery and a single oral dose before discharge is enough. Infants under 60 weeks postmenstrual age get paracetamol and the block only.

Rebound is moderate: the block ends in the early evening at home. Parents who have been told to give the first oral doses on the clock, without waiting for the child to complain, rarely have a bad night. Pain that escalates on day 2-3 after a groin operation suggests haematoma or infection; a swollen, tender testis after orchidopexy suggests torsion or ischaemia.

## Pitfalls and variants

- The ex-premature infant. Apnoea is the risk, prematurity and anaemia the predictors, and overnight monitoring the plan with either technique. Intravenous caffeine is used by some units as prophylaxis on the basis of small trials; it does not replace monitoring.
- Awake spinal. About one in five infants allocated to it in the trial had a protocol violation, which in practice means a block that failed or ran out. Have a plan for conversion that is not a hurried gas induction in a baby held in the lateral position with the abdomen open.
- Incarcerated hernia. Reduced under sedation and analgesia by the surgeon, then repaired after a day or two; if irreducible, an emergency with obstruction, a full stomach, dehydration and oedematous tissue that makes the dissection longer.
- Girls. The sac may contain ovary; the operation is otherwise the same and there is no cord, so an ilioinguinal block covers it.
- Laparoscopic repair. A different anaesthetic (tube, relaxant, pneumoperitoneum) for the same pathology; surgeons vary by training.
- Impalpable testis. Diagnostic laparoscopy, then either a one-stage laparoscopic orchidopexy or division of the testicular vessels with a second stage at about six months: two anaesthetics, each needing port-site infiltration and a caudal.
- Caudal contraindications and failures. A sacral dimple or tuft needs imaging first. Subcutaneous injection is the usual failure; intravascular and intraosseous injection are the dangerous ones in infants, hence slow fractionated dosing with the ECG in view.
- Femoral block after ilioinguinal injection. Seen with landmark technique and larger volumes: the child cannot stand at discharge. It resolves; warn the parents.
- Hypospadias repair. Longer surgery on the same territory with a catheter and dressings; a caudal is standard and the plan otherwise follows this card. Adult equivalents are in [scrotal and penile surgery](scrotal-and-penile-surgery.html) and [inguinal and femoral hernia repair](inguinal-and-femoral-hernia-repair-open.html).
- Doses, fasting and airway sizes by weight are in [the child](../a/a13-the-child.html); day-case criteria are in [nausea, vomiting and day-case anaesthesia](../a/a11-ponv-day-case.html).

## Key sources

This card rests on one large randomised trial and its two follow-ups for the infant hernia question, a combined analysis from 1995 for the age thresholds of apnoea risk, a Cochrane review for circumcision, a registry for block safety and a single randomised trial for ultrasound guidance. The admission thresholds (60 weeks postmenstrual age for ex-premature infants, 44-46 weeks for term infants) are institutional conventions derived from the 1995 analysis and vary between units. Caudal volumes follow the long-established weight-based scheme and are stated without a source, as are the clonidine dose, the penile block volume, the 20-30% landmark failure rate quoted in the ultrasound trial's introduction, and the use of caffeine. The comparison of caudal with ilioinguinal block for herniotomy and orchidopexy was not verified against a specific meta-analysis during drafting; the recommendation is argued from innervation. No PROSPECT guideline exists for paediatric groin surgery, and the 179-procedure pain cohort was in adults.
