GLP-1 medications before surgery

Ozempic, Mounjaro and similar medicines slow your stomach down. Food can still be in your stomach the next day. So your fast starts the day before, and it is different from the usual fasting rules.

Why your fast is longer

An anaesthetic is safest on an empty stomach. If food is still there, it can come back up while you are asleep. These medicines keep food in your stomach for longer, so you stop food much earlier.

What clear broth means

A thin, watery soup you can see through, with no bits and no cream. Any of these is fine:

  • Clear chicken or beef broth from the shop, heated up
  • Stock powder (such as Massel or Continental) mixed with hot water
  • Tinned consommé, if it is fully clear

Before you drink it, always strain it, even if it came from the shop. Pour it through a fine sieve or tea strainer into your cup or bowl, so any bits stay behind.

Not pumpkin soup, chicken noodle soup, cream of chicken, or miso with bits in it.

Keep taking your medicine

Do not stop it unless your doctor tells you to. If you are unsure, contact your doctor or Dr Halvey's rooms before your operation.

Rybelsus tablets: same fluid rules, and do not take your tablet on the morning of surgery. Start it again when you are eating and drinking normally.

Ate something, or could not stick to clear fluids for the full 24 hours? Tell your doctor or anaesthetist as early as you can. They may delay your surgery, take extra steps to lower the risk during your anaesthetic, or give you a medicine to help empty your stomach. It is always safer to tell us.

If your stomach is slow for another reason

Gastroparesis, Parkinson's disease and some bowel problems can slow your stomach even when you have fasted correctly. Tell your doctor or anaesthetist about these before your surgery.

Where this guidance comes from

The national guideline published in April 2025, endorsed by ANZCA (the Australian and New Zealand College of Anaesthetists), the Australian Diabetes Society, GESA and NACOS. Read the guideline on the ANZCA website.
