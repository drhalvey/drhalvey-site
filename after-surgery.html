After your anaesthetic

This page covers what is normal after an anaesthetic, what helps, when to get help and who to call. If you were given a code for a personal pain relief plan, you can open it from here too.

Take pain relief on time

Regular medicines work best taken by the clock, not when pain arrives.

Before a block wears off

Start your tablets while your arm or leg is still numb.

Tell someone early

Sickness, itch and pain are easier to treat before they build up.

Know who to call

The ward, the rooms, healthdirect, or 000 in an emergency.

What is normal after an anaesthetic

What you may noticeWhat helps
DrowsinessRest. Do not drive, use machinery, sign important documents or drink alcohol for 24 hours after a general anaesthetic or sedation.
Sore or scratchy throatCommon after a breathing tube or airway. Cold drinks and throat lozenges help. It settles on its own.
Feeling sickSip fluids, eat small plain meals and move slowly. Take any anti-sickness medicine you were given. Tell the ward or the rooms if you cannot keep fluids down.
ItchingCommon after morphine in a spinal. It is not an allergy and fades as the morphine wears off. Tell the nurses if it is bothering you.
Shivering or feeling coldWarm blankets and warm drinks. It settles as you warm up.
Trouble passing urineCommon after a spinal or some pain medicines. The nurses may scan your bladder and occasionally pass a catheter.
A numb, heavy arm or legExpected while a nerve block works. Protect it from heat and pressure, and take your pain relief before the feeling returns. See nerve blocks.
A sore or bruised backCan happen after a spinal or epidural. Simple pain relief helps. Tell your GP if it is getting worse rather than better.
ConstipationCommon with strong pain medicines. Drink plenty, move when you can, and use the laxative you were given.

Pain relief at home

  • Take your regular medicines, such as paracetamol and an anti-inflammatory if you were given one, on time, even if you feel comfortable.
  • If you had a nerve block, take your first doses before the numbness fades, often in the evening or overnight.
  • Use the stronger medicine you were given only when the regular medicines are not enough, and reduce it as you improve.
  • Never take more than the dose on the label, and do not drink alcohol with strong pain medicines.

When to get help

Call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department if you have:
  • chest pain, shortness of breath or trouble breathing
  • severe drowsiness, or you are hard to wake
  • new weakness in your legs, or problems controlling your bladder or bowels after a spinal or epidural.

Contact your surgeon, the hospital or the rooms the same day if you have:

  • pain that keeps getting worse despite your medicines, especially with a tight cast, splint or dressing
  • vomiting that will not settle, or you cannot keep fluids down
  • a fever, or redness, swelling or discharge from the wound
  • numbness or weakness that has not started to wear off by the time you were told, or comes back after it went
  • a severe headache that is worse when you sit or stand, after a spinal or epidural
  • pain or swelling in your calf.
